## Context

### The owner's decisions (2026-10-08)

The owner decides; there is no counsel.

1. **Danger stays with the AI.** A person in crisis is a lead; the owner does not treat crisis. Danger gets the site's text, the referral to CVV (188): no handover, no exception of its own. The owner also wants it warmer and more human: two fixed texts the owner approves (`safety` for a conversation's first crisis message, `safety_followup` after it), and a crisis conversation in which a model reads the crisis message to lead the person to the CVV (§I), chosen knowing it is an exception to W1.
2. **After a crisis, keep answering, never book.** The safety text goes again on every crisis message; a booking request goes to the owner's waiting list. The rule is delegated ("decide o melhor").
3. **Automatic sending, delegated:** "o que for melhor para o sistema, se a IA der conta, ela que faça".
4. **Every new WhatsApp number becomes a CRM lead automatically, and the AI answers it.** Confirmed ("Sim, automático"): ADR 0025 B1 is (c), replacing that morning's (b).
5. **The site's triage** (a prefilled message with the demand, how long, a 0-10 rating) is kept for the owner; the AI never reads it (W1) and knows only that it was done. The owner reads these as lead-qualification and awareness questions, not a health triage ("é mais uma triagem de qualificacao de lead"), keeps the site's message as it is, and holds that the AI may ask them on WhatsApp. Under W1 a model still never reads an answer's health content (§G, §H).
6. **A request for a person** joins a first-in, first-out waiting list; the owner is notified on the owner's own WhatsApp number (name and time, never content) and replies from the system.
7. **Patients:** confirmation, a reminder 5 hours before, the Meet link 5 minutes before; clinical conversation stays outside the system.
8. No team `handoffNames` for now.
9. **A contact who asked to stop and writes again** takes on new messages: the receptionist answers normally, and a crisis gets the CVV text like anyone else's. This replaces ADR 0025 B7 (b) for the contact's own opt-out (§C).

**W1 holds:** no health information reaches AI through OpenRouter (SI-78, SI-80), with one exception the owner chose: the crisis conversation (§I). **Delegation:** each item whose recommendation was an engineering or product default is decided by delegation; an owner's fact or policy stays an open decision or a slice's need.

### Where the system stands (`feature/clinical-phase-1` at `1bcefd50`)

- **Danger is integrated (PR #33).** `948a0e69`: a crisis keeps the conversation with the agent and raises no exception, A3 never holds it, and a release is refused only while an opt-out is open. `4aecf2a8`: the earlier turns show the safety and `sensitive_only_*` texts only as the marker (a deny-list). Not done: the safety text in a held conversation; the review's "A person should look at this conversation now." ADR 0025 A2 was corrected in `b35d4832`.
- **Fixed texts** are pending reviews, sent only by a person's accept and `messaging send`, the Meta token's only holder.
- **Person replies** anchor only on a message held for a person, which alone keeps them out of the model's earlier turns (keyed on the screening, not the author). A non-text message (audio, image) is refused on the record: no task, counted nowhere, every earlier reply stale (SI-81).
- **New numbers:** W6 (a); the admission's do-not-contact snapshot is immutable. **Triage:** packs v2 to v4 omit it; nothing stores it. **Booking:** the AI never books but sees up to ten free slots. **Real data:** Q8 refuses a `health` run (SI-70); production channels stay inactive (SI-47).

One record: these capabilities share one machine (a worker job carrying a send the database authorized) and the same functions.

### What Meta says (verified 2026-10-08)

- **Window:** a user's message opens a 24-hour customer-service window: free-form messages inside; outside, only approved templates to users who opted in. Inside it, non-template messages and utility templates are free; marketing and authentication templates are charged even there.
- **Templates:** review within 24 hours, which can reject; Meta may recategorize utility as marketing; parameters need examples and may not open or close the text.
- **Webhooks** carry `contacts[].profile.name`, `wa_id` and `messages[].from`; since April 2026 also a BSUID, and `from` may be absent for a username-only user.
- **Business Messaging Policy** (updated 2026-09-23): automation inside the window needs "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths" to a person; and "Don't use WhatsApp for telemedicine or to send or request any health related information" where regulations prohibit sending it to systems without heightened safeguards. The site's prefilled triage carries a demand, a duration and a rating. The owner reads it as lead qualification and keeps it (decision 5); that reading of Meta's policy, and its risk, are the owner's.

**To verify:** an owner alert as a utility template, and its rate; template parameter limits and permanent errors; whether a development-mode number sends a custom template; the `wa.me/?text=` format; Brazil's ninth digit in `wa_id`; referral fields (B4); when sending to a BSUID starts; the terms on general-purpose AI chatbots; redelivery up to 7 days (ADR 0018 cites no Meta page); how the duty to honour a request to stop applies to the contact's own later message.

## Decision

### Principles

- **W1:** nothing adds health content, or a crisis-derived signal, to a model's input, except the crisis conversation's own contract (§I).
- **Deterministic code decides:** a model's output never sends, raises or resolves an exception, sets a hold or writes the CRM (SI-45, SI-82).
- **Every send keeps every gate** (SI-49, SI-50, SI-81), at most one provider call, with one exception: SI-49's "exactly one CRM contact" is relaxed for the safety texts only (§B), and so is its opt-out gate when the opt-out is one a person set, since a contact's own later message lifts the contact's own opt-out (decision 9, §C).
- **The database authorizes, the worker carries, a person asks;** no browser or handler holds the Meta token.
- **Test and synthetic data only** until the production gate's ADR (SI-47). **Tenant words are data:** texts, templates and placeholders are owner configuration.

### A. Who wrote a review, and a person's reply to the newest message

- **Authorship.** `review_items.author`: `agent`, `fixed` or `person`; immutable, backfilled exactly from each run's ending. An insert guard binds it: `agent` never on a run the front desk settled with a fixed text or a hold (the earlier turns show an agent reply only when its run's screening went to the model, so a test fixture's review on another run stays harmless); `fixed` only on a run cancelled `front_desk_fixed_reply` with a `fixed_reply` screening; `person` only inside `ops.record_person_reply` or the inbox's reply (a transaction-local mark they set). Built in slice 1 (migration `20261017120000_review_authorship.sql`).
- **The earlier turns become an allowlist keyed on author:** an `agent` reply verbatim; a `fixed` text verbatim only for `clarification`, `human_handoff_ack` (never one the scheduling hold caused, §F) and `opt_out_ack`; anything else, a person's reply included, as the neutral marker. This replaces `4aecf2a8`'s deny-list: a key added later stays hidden.
- **A person's reply** anchors on the newest admitted message, whatever its disposition, while the conversation's 24-hour window is open (a refused message also opens it). The revision is the count of the contact's messages, admitted or refused (`ops.cos_conversation_revision`): local to the conversation and only growing, so it is safe to print (SI-26). It is stale only when a message, admitted or refused, arrived after the conversation revision the person saw (the inbox's token, or the one `front-desk conversations` prints and `front-desk reply` names); a refused message inside it does not. A refused message in a held conversation counts on `message_waiting` (`ops.receive_whatsapp_message`).
- **One non-`person` review per run** (a partial unique index; every `on conflict` names its predicate); up to 5 person reviews per anchor and revision *(delegated)*: a new message from the contact, even a refused one, opens room for five more. A run still waiting for its screening (an execution stop holds it, or no worker took it yet) does not refuse the person; its later screening, under a person, drafts at most a protective text. A draft the agent or a fixed text wrote for the message is stale once a person replied to it, but for the safety text and the opt-out acknowledgement, which only the contact's next message makes stale; the review page says which (`author`, `answeredByPerson`). Review events are keyed on the review (`review:<review id>:pending`), since `ops.record_event` refuses a second review's event under the run's key; every lookup by run (the post-settlement opener, triage recover, `ops.request_shadow_decision_for_settled_job`, structured decisions) selects the non-person review, `strict`. No review opens on a redacted task (SI-72).
- **Protective texts whoever holds** *(delegated)*: `safety`, `safety_followup` and `opt_out_ack` are chosen before the holder check; the message still counts as waiting.
- **Wording:** "Send the safety text."; for `opt_out_ack`, "Send the acknowledgement, then record the opt-out in the CRM." until slice 3, whose migration queues the CRM's opt-out record and then says "Send the acknowledgement; the opt-out is recorded after it."
- **With no fixed texts published (m1),** every message that needs a fixed text, a crisis included, goes to a person (`configuration_missing`); the others still reach the model.
- **Tests, failing by name:** a person's reply on a model run or a `human_handoff_ack` run never reaches the provider; an audio after a request for a person, then a person's reply that leaves; a person's reply to a `fixed_reply` run records its event and asks no second structured decision; each author-guard mutant; a crisis in a held conversation gets its safety review.

### B. Published fixed texts leave on their own (decision 3)

AI-written replies stay supervised; their autonomy needs its own ADR, once the supervised live test shows the AI can handle it.

**Built in slice 2** (migration `20261018120000_automatic_fixed_texts.sql`, SI-83). The construction reconciled five points the map of the code found, recorded here:
- **One safety family.** `safety` and `safety_followup` are one family for staleness: only a newer automatic text of the family makes one stale, so two close crisis messages send one text, the second. `safety_followup` is chosen when an earlier screening of the conversation already selected a safety text and the published set holds it; it is optional in a published set, so stored sets stay valid.
- **The opt-out rule** follows decision 9: an open opt-out stops every policy send but its own acknowledgement; a safety text only an opt-out screened on a message newer than its own.
- **The safety texts' contact** may also be a CRM the tenant cannot read (`crm_unavailable`); never an ambiguous or erased number, never past the window.
- **Only test or synthetic data** is authorized, while BASELINE Q8 is open (the predicate reads the task's data class).
- **The waits** are the reply job's own: the begin capability gives the job back (`released`, attempt restored, at most 30 seconds or the freshness bound), because `ops.defer_job` defers only under a stop. The begin capability also blocks the send once the bound passes, so three capabilities suffice (begin, confirm, settle) plus the post-settlement exceptions step; there is no separate defer capability.
- **The screening authorizes only what the send would let leave now:** a contact the send would refuse leaves the review pending for a person (its contact exception already says why), and a stop holds the send instead of refusing it.

The branch's adversarial review (four lenses, each finding verified) confirmed nine findings, all answered before its PR:
- **The order of the gates.** The send's gates judge the contact, then the opt-out rule, and the kill switch last, so a stop never hides a contact the send would refuse. The begin capability settles a text a newer message replaced as stale before anything else, so it never raises `send_blocked`.
- **The kill switch is read again at the last gate** (the automated review of PR #35, P1; migration `20261018130000_reply_send_stop_recheck.sql`): a stop tripped after the send began holds it, never called, immediately before the call. The lock is a session lock for the read alone, given back before the call, so a stop's trip never waits for a call in flight.
- **The last gate reads again what can change before the call.** It waits for the conversation at most five seconds (never more than half the statement's own bound); when it cannot take it, or an acknowledgement's newer message is still to be screened, it returns the send to authorized, never called, and the job to the queue (the one edge back the send's guard allows, for a job's send only); a number erased since the send began settles it failed. A call the deadline stopped before it started is `failed`, not `indeterminate`.
- **What the cap counts:** only texts that left, may have left, or are authorized and not replaced, so a burst of messages answered by one text is not a loop.
- **A job that ends without settling its send** (its attempts spent, or its worker dead on the last one) is closed out by the worker's reaper tick (`ops.settle_stale_reply_sends`): blocked `job_failed` and listed when it never began, `indeterminate` when it may have left. The owner's sync also blocks an out-of-date text whose job is still queued; only a live lease is left alone.

**Provenance.** `ops.review_is_published_fixed_text(review)` holds exactly SI-83's conditions (the key K must be in `automaticFixedTexts` of the policy version the screening recorded and of the one published when the send begins, m3; for `safety`, eligibility judges the contact afresh) and only on a task not redacted.

**Authorization as policy.** When K is automatic, `ops.authorize_fixed_reply`, in the screening's transaction, records `decision_basis = published_fixed_text` with reviewer `policy:fixed-text`, requests the send (`authorization_kind = fixed_text`, key, version, job id) and queues one `outbound.reply_send` job. `ENABLE ALWAYS` guards check the predicate on every such row (the review's update guard and the send's insert guard); a send with a `job_id` is `fixed_text` (slice 5 adds `person_reply`, whose review's author is `person`), so a model-written reply leaves only by the operator's send, which refuses a send a job carries and a review the policy accepted. A person's decision records its basis (`person`) and can never name `policy:` or `system:`. The browser shows the fixed text's own label; a "Texto fixo publicado" mark on the review page waits for slice 5's inbox.

**The switch:** the operating policy's `automaticFixedTexts`, a closed array of fixed-text keys (absent: supervised); no field can name a model's output; `sendMode` stays unread *(delegated)*. First list *(delegated)*: `safety`, `safety_followup`, `human_handoff_ack`, `opt_out_ack`, `clarification`, `sensitive_only_*`.

**Two safety texts** (decision 1): `safety` for a conversation's first crisis message, and `safety_followup`, a different text the owner approves, for each later one. Both carry the CVV referral and share every rule of `safety` in this record.

**The safety text's contact** *(delegated)* may also be a number the CRM does not know (`not_found`, `possible_match`, a capped or failed creation) or a contact with no lead profile (`consent_unknown`); never an ambiguous number. A recorded opt-out does not stop it (decision 9): the crisis message itself lifts a contact's own opt-out (§C), and an opt-out a person set still lets the safety texts through. This relaxes SI-49 for the safety texts only.

**The reply-send job** *(delegated: the worker, for the kill switch and a `job_kind` stop)*. `outbound.reply_send` is external and carries exactly one send the database authorized before the job existed (`fixed_text`, or §E's `person_reply`), never an AI draft: not ADR 0018's rejected generic kind. Every stop scope holds it (SI-37); the lease resolves the send by `job_id` (SI-30). A held-call variant of `engine/worker/externalCall.ts`, through four pinned lease-bound capabilities taking no tenant, review or text:
- **TX2a** `beginReplySend` re-checks the authority, eligibility under the kill-switch lock, redaction, the stale rule, freshness and the privacy notice, and commits `sending`; a block is recorded, never raised.
- **TX2b** `confirmReplySend` locks the conversation (SI-81), re-reads the predicate and makes the one call inside the transaction (at most 60 s); `settleReplySend` records the outcome; exceptions sync after. An attempt finding its send `sending` records it `indeterminate` without calling.
- **No transport:** `deferReplySend` defers the job, attempt restored, until its bound passes (a fixed text's freshness, a person reply's window), then blocks the send `transport_not_configured` and raises `send_blocked`; tested, so no send waits forever.

**Transports:** `meta`, with `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN` read at start by no handler; `fake` only under `DEPLOYMENT_ENVIRONMENT=local`; each send records its transport. On staging, only a token whose system user holds the test WhatsApp Business Account alone: today's `atomic-crm` token also holds the clinic number's account, so the worker never gets it (slice 2).

**Pre-checks:** channel `85ae4f8b…` (the clinic number) is deactivated before any key is listed; pre-checks move to the test-number channel under a worker without a transport, so their texts end `transport_not_configured`, or reach only the owner's registered device from Meta's test number if a token-holding worker runs within the bound.

**Safeguards** *(delegated)*:
- **Freshness:** a text not begun within 30 minutes of the earlier of its message's provider timestamp and admission instant is blocked `fixed_text_expired`; a job's window reads the same instant.
- **Cap:** 3 automatic texts per conversation per hour, safety exempt; past it a person holds the conversation (`automatic_cap`), ending auto-responder loops.
- **Acknowledgements answer the request, not the words:** only a newer automatic text of the same key makes `safety`, `human_handoff_ack` or `opt_out_ack` stale. Its job waits, within the freshness bound, while a newer message is neither screened nor ended (M2), so a newer opt-out blocks it and two close crisis messages send one text (m2). Other keys keep SI-81.
- **Nothing fails silently:** a refused or blocked automatic text raises `send_blocked` (high) on the send, or on the conversation when no send row exists; never for `newer_message`, never naming the text.
- **The privacy notice** still rides the first reply, safety included (SI-79), and names how to reach a person, the escalation path Meta requires (the owner writes it).

**Gateway residual (M10).** ADR 0018 says a compromised gateway can never make a send. Now a forged call or leaked app secret can, for a registered test sender on a test channel: cause one fixed text per forged message (safety exempt from the cap), create a lead (§C), set a scheduling hold that never expires (§F), write `do_not_contact` through a forged opt-out (§C), lift that sender's own recorded opt-out through a forged message that is not an opt-out (§C; never a flag a person set), after which automatic fixed texts and accepted replies can reach the number again, and notify the owner through a forged request for a person, within the caps (§D). Never a model-written or person reply, a booking or a resend.

### C. Every new number becomes a lead; the opt-out reaches the CRM (decision 4)

**The lead, inside the admission:** in `ops.receive_whatsapp_message`, after the conversation upsert and before the CRM lookup, so the snapshot reads `found` and the first message is answered.
- **When:** only on `not_found`; never on a redelivery of an id already admitted or refused, nor for a null sender (username-only stays refused). While Q8 and the production gate are closed, only for a registered test sender on a test channel *(delegated)*; a message refused on the record creates it too.
- **Failures and races:** a sub-block with `lock_timeout = '2s'`; a transient failure (lock, serialization, deadlock, timeout, cancellation) answers 500 so Meta redelivers (SI-53); any other error falls back to `contact_unresolved`. A conversation serializes on the upsert, a number on two channels on an advisory lock; a person creating the same number at that moment yields `ambiguous` until merged.
- **Adapter** `ops.crm_create_whatsapp_lead` (SI-68 shape, executable by no role): one contact (phone `+` and Meta's digits, no country code guessed), the lead profile the CRM's trigger adds, one `whatsapp` attribution; it answers `created`, `existing`, `ambiguous`, `unavailable` or `skipped:<reason>`; only `created` uses the conversation's one creation slot.
- **Near-duplicate guard** *(delegated)*: a contact whose digits end with the sender's last eight blocks creation and raises `contact_unresolved` (`possible_match`); it shares the exact match's single pass.
- **Name** *(delegated)*: `contacts[].profile.name`, chosen by the sender: bounded, control characters and a leading `= + - @` stripped, screened with the agent's pack and used only when benign with nothing omitted, else the owner's placeholder (default `Contato WhatsApp`); first name at creation only, in no event, log, ledger or model. The receive function's sixth parameter has a default; `sales_id` stays null.
- **Cap** *(delegated)*: 50 creations per tenant per day, recorded by the owner's tool; past it `skipped:cap`; with no cap recorded, nothing is created (`skipped:no_cap`).
- **Ledger and event:** `ops.crm_contact_acts` (append-only, no number or name) records each act and skip; `lead.created` carries the conversation and channel.

**The opt-out reaches the CRM after its acknowledgement.** Every screening that detects an opt-out, whoever holds, queues one internal `crm.opt_out_record` job due at the window's end, moved to now when the acknowledgement settles terminal or blocked. It is the worker's one CRM write path: `ops.crm_record_opt_out` resolves the contact afresh, sets `lead_profiles.do_not_contact = true` and nothing else, records the act and `lead.opted_out`, and reconciles `opt_out`; an ambiguous or missing contact leaves it open. No stop holds it (an opt-out is protective); until then eligibility refuses every send but the acknowledgement. The gateway records no opt-out.

**The consent ledger** *(delegated)*: every change of the opt-out flag, the CRM form's included, is recorded append-only on SI-66's pattern.

**The contact's own later message lifts the contact's own opt-out** (decision 9, replacing ADR 0025 B7 (b)). The admission records whether the CRM's flag is the contact's own system-recorded opt-out or one a person set. A later message from that contact that is not itself an opt-out, admitted after the opt-out was recorded in the CRM, is screened as usual and queues one internal `crm.opt_out_lift` job: `ops.crm_lift_opt_out` clears the flag, records that message's reference as the reason in the consent ledger and `lead.opt_out_lifted`, and nothing else; the reply's eligibility then reads the CRM afresh. A flag a person set is never lifted by a message: A3 keeps holding those (`do_not_contact`), but the safety texts. A `lead_profiles` trigger refuses a true-to-false change from any application role while the ledger's latest entry is a system-recorded opt-out, so the CRM form (`LeadCommercialPanel.tsx`) can set the flag but not clear the contact's own.

**The CRM copy follows the number's retention** *(delegated)*: when a number is tombstoned (SI-79), the identifier job's capability or the owner's erasure deletes a system-created contact no one edited (an `AFTER UPDATE` trigger on SI-66's pattern records a content-free edit) with no deal (`deals.contact_ids`), note, task or recorded opt-out; one a person worked on stays under the CRM's lifecycle, as the notice will say.

**Built in slice 3** (migrations `20261019120000_whatsapp_leads.sql`, `20261019130000_crm_consent_and_opt_out.sql`, `20261019140000_opt_out_lift.sql`, `20261019150000_crm_copy_retention.sql` and `20261019160000_slice3_review_fixes.sql`; SI-84 added, its final text in docs/SECURITY_INVARIANTS.md). An adversarial review of the design (25 findings, each verified) changed it before any code; an adversarial review of the built branch (five lenses, 28 findings, 25 confirmed or plausible and answered, 3 refuted) changed it again, through the last migration, `merge_contacts`' locks and the tests it named. Together they reconciled these points:
- **The lead.** The adapter runs only on `not_found`, for a registered test sender, and records only its creations and its skips (`created`, `skipped:cap`, `skipped:no_cap`, `skipped:possible_match`, `skipped:error`); a duplicate delivery re-reads what the first one made of the message before the lead step. The cap is the owner's lead policy (`npm run front-desk -- lead-policy record`, with its IANA time zone and placeholder); with none in force nothing is created. The name is screened by the gateway with the newest reviewed pack (not the agent's), refused when it carries a digit, an `@` or a link, and the database checks the first word again.
- **The opt-out hold.** A message that arrives before the opt-out is recorded waits with the person who holds the conversation, as any held message does; nothing supersedes a pending opt-out by itself. A person who reads the opt-out as a false positive dismisses its `opt_out` episode: the record job then writes nothing (`dismissed`) and the episode's unsent automatic acknowledgement becomes stale. The record waits while the acknowledgement is still on its way (at most until it is out of date). Only an acknowledgement a person accepted brings the record forward when it settles (an operator's send settles on the provider's status callback); an automatic acknowledgement leaves it at the window's end, so a person can still dismiss an opt-out the screen read wrongly ('me tira uma dúvida'), and a dismissal committing while the record runs is read under the episode's lock. A number's erasure records its pending opt-out first, while the number still resolves the contact.
- **The consent ledger** (`public.lead_consent_changes`) records the system's origin only when a backend adapter marked the write and named the message; a person's write naming the flag with true is recorded even when unchanged. The merge writes the flag only when the loser opted out, so a winner's own opt-out keeps its origin. The merge holds both contacts and both lead profiles before it reads them, so an opt-out recorded or lifted meanwhile is folded in, never cascaded away; a record or lift that finds its contact gone tries again and resolves the number anew. Residual: a merge that folds in another contact's opt-out makes the winner's flag a person's: a message then lifts only the contact's own opt-out, and a person clears the flag.
- **The lift is part of the screening, not a job.** It reads the CRM as it is when the message is screened and the order of the messages, not an admission snapshot (no admission column was added): it clears the flag only when the ledger's newest entry is the system's record of an opt-out from an older message and no newer message of the number asked to stop. So a message admitted before the record and screened after it lifts it, a message from before the opt-out never does, and an older message never undoes a newer opt-out. A lifted message is reachable (the screening, the reviews it opens and the policy's predicate read the effective flag), and the conversation the opt-out handed to a person goes back to the agent unless a person took it over or replied since, or another reason for a person is open. A flag a person set is never cleared by a message: when a person's flag is part of the opt-out (a person turned it on or named it on again, or it was on before the ledger existed), the lift records the contact's own opt-out as lifted (an entry that leaves the flag on) and the flag stays for the person to clear. A person who took over a conversation held for another reason is recorded as holding it, so a later message never gives it back. For a crisis message no failure of the lift holds the screening; any other message waits for the profile's lock and its run retries. The screening takes the conversation before the CRM, the order an erasure takes.
- **The CRM form's guard** refuses a true-to-false change of the contact's own system-recorded opt-out from every writer but the lift's own mark (`OS403`, its own message), the owner in SQL included, and a person naming the flag on again first takes nothing over; a flag a person set the form can still clear. Outside the guard: renumbering a contact, deleting and re-inserting its lead profile as `service_role`, an owner-set lift mark, and a person deleting the contact (the number's next conversation then creates a lead without the opt-out).
- **The CRM copy's retention.** A person's work is marked in `public.crm_contact_edits` (any write to the contact, its lead profile or its attributions that no adapter marked as its own; the three adapters mark theirs). An erasure asks about every contact its conversation created or its admissions found, so a lead found by a second conversation goes once both numbers are erased, whichever first; the adapter holds the contact, its profile and its attributions before any check, so a person's note written meanwhile keeps the contact instead of being cascaded; an opt-out keeps the contact only while in force; an erasure by number holds every conversation of the number, in id order, before any CRM row, so a screening, a record or an identifier job on its second conversation never waits on it in a cycle. Residual: a deal created at that instant can still name a deleted id (deals carry no foreign key).
- **Follow-up, not in this slice:** the operator's send (`npm run messaging -- send`) reads the CRM's flag afresh but not an open `opt_out` episode, so between an opt-out and its record a person could still send a reply accepted earlier; it is recorded as a follow-up for the send's eligibility.
- **Deployment order:** stop every worker before applying `20261019130000`, and restart workers only from the integrated code: a worker without the `crm.opt_out_record` handler fails that job permanently, and the opt-out is then not recorded until the contact opts out again or the number is erased.
- **PR #36 review (Codex):** P1, a person naming the flag on again (the CRM saving the profile unchanged) hid the contact's own opt-out from its lift while the form's clear guard still refused a clear, so the flag stayed on for good: the lift now skips the same entries the guard skips (`20261019170000_opt_out_lift_person_resave.sql`, `crm_consent.sql` C7, red on the earlier definition); SI-84 says "the newest entry, a person naming the flag on again aside". P2, a deal saved in the instant an erasure deletes its lead can still name the deleted id: already this slice's recorded residual, not changed.

### D. The waiting list and the owner's notification (decision 6)

- **The waiting list,** read-only in the existing overview: open `person_requested` and `message_waiting` episodes, first in, first out, with an opaque reference, kinds, age, count, the window's deadline and the last notification's state; no text, number or name.
- **The target** (`ops.owner_notification_targets`: channel, digits, templates, kinds, quiet hours, caps) is recorded only by the owner's act, `front-desk -- notify-target record|retire`; never seeded or printed.
- **The intent:** when `ops.open_exception` opens a `person_requested` episode, or counts the first waiting message of a holding episode or the first after each person reply, the same transaction inserts one `ops.owner_notifications` row (unique per episode and per person reply it follows) and one `owner_notification.send` job, due after a 60-second debounce or at quiet hours' end. Never for a failure or a crisis message.
- **The job** (external, held by the kill switch) prepares (stops, target, active test channel, caps; `skipped_resolved` if the episode closed; waiting episodes coalesced into a digest), calls once, never retried.
- **Content:** two utility templates, one episode ("a person asked to talk to you", first name, time) and a digest (a count); the first name, read at prepare, is cut to the letters and spaces of its first word, at most 20 characters, stored nowhere.
- **Defaults** *(delegated)*: quiet hours 22:00 to 08:00 (America/Sao_Paulo) with a morning digest; 10 per hour, 30 per day; no e-mail; no window-reopening template for now (revisit after the first weeks on test data).
- **After danger** *(delegated)*: a crisis message in a held conversation still counts on `message_waiting`, and a blocked safety text raises `send_blocked`; both are content-free and neither notifies.
- **The owner's number** is recognised at admission before the conversation upsert, so no conversation row holds it, and becomes a content-free fact (no task, model or lead); a registered test sender takes precedence on a test channel, whose conversation holds it as today. It lands before lead creation leaves test senders.

**Built in slice 4** (migrations `20261020120000_owner_notifications.sql` and `20261020130000_waiting_list_read_model.sql`; SI-86 added, SI-48 and SI-82 amended, their final texts in docs/SECURITY_INVARIANTS.md). An adversarial review of the design (22 findings, each verified) changed it before any code; an adversarial review of the built branch (five lenses, 22 findings, 21 confirmed and 1 plausible, all P2, all answered) changed it again. Together they reconciled these points:
- **The target is fixed on the intent,** with the unit of the target's channel, when the intent is recorded. A notification whose target was replaced or retired before it left is blocked (`target_changed`, `no_target`), never sent to the new number; a send carries only due intents of its own target. The owner's act checks every value first and answers with fixed messages that never carry one (a constraint's refusal is answered the same way), refuses a number any conversation or CRM contact holds in either Brazil mobile form (a CRM contact also by its last eight digits, so a lead saved without the country code is still a lead), exempting a conversation only where the number is a sender the owner registered on that conversation's channel and a CRM contact only where it is one on the target's channel, the gateway's own per-channel rule; it says how to erase a held number (`npm run ops -- identifiers erase --number-file <path>`, once for each form). The number reaches the commands only from a file (`--number-file`, its whole content, UTF-8 or the UTF-16 Windows PowerShell writes), and a usage message of either tool withholds any run of six digits. The fallback word is letters in single-spaced words, at most 20, which the template transport always accepts.
- **Only what reached or may reach the owner holds a gap.** The once-per-gap rule and the uniqueness backstop count intents pending, sending, sent, delivered, read, indeterminate, or carried by such a send; a failed send fails what it carried (`carrier_failed`), so the next waiting message may tell the owner again, and the caps bound those retries. Delivery news after a failure records its instant and the send stays failed, so it never takes back a gap a later message used (which would also break the uniqueness backstop). Residual: such a send's conversations can be told a second time.
- **Waiting, never dropped:** quiet hours and the caps give the job back until the exact instant they end; a worker without a transport gives it back every 30 seconds until the intent expires after 24 hours (it is never blocked for that). Contingency without template sending: retire the target (or never record it); never unset `REPLY_TRANSPORT`, which the automatic texts need.
- **The send's lock order:** the tenant's notification lock, then the notification's own row, the kill switch, then the batch (its due rows of the same target that no stop holds, in the order they were recorded, so a stop on an older intent's unit is never bypassed after the channel moves to another agent) and their episodes, taken without waiting: a release or a screening holding one makes the send wait five seconds instead of joining a lock cycle, and a release committing first is seen and sets its intent aside. The intent itself never waits more than 500 ms for a lock; a lock wait or any other failure of it is a warning carrying only its SQLSTATE, and the admission or screening goes on. Residual: a screening and a gateway admission of one conversation at the same instant can each record an intent on its own episode; the send then carries both in one notification.
- **The provider's message id names its recipient,** so it is kept only as its SHA-256 digest; a status is matched by that digest, or, for an indeterminate send only, by its correlation (`owner-notification:<id>`) and a recipient that is the target's number, compared in SQL and never returned. A status for a send still settling (its digest is written only when it settles) is refused as transient, so the gateway answers 500 and Meta delivers it again; bounded to five minutes, so a send a dead worker left is never waited for. The gateway holds the owner's acts on the target off while it recognises the number (a per-tenant advisory lock, shared by the gateway, exclusive for the acts).
- **The instant the owner reads** is stored on the intent (`waiting_since`: the screened message's own admission instant, or the refused message's arrival as the conversation records it, its last message's, read without any session setting the gateway's entry points never touch), and the episode template shows the oldest of the send's.
- **The transport:** approved templates ride the worker's one reply transport (`ReplyTransport.templates`, an `OutboundTemplateTransport`; `OutboundTransport` is unchanged). Meta's template payload and response were verified on its pages; where `biz_opaque_callback_data` goes in a template send, the parameters' whitespace rules and the template error codes are settled by the live probe.
- **The browser** reads the waiting list as an optional key of the existing overview, so the frontend ships before the migration that adds it; the section is hidden while the database does not carry it.
- **Deployment order:** worker, frontend, database. Staging: the owner's registered device can be the target (a registered sender takes precedence at admission, so its own messages stay a test lead); the two utility templates must be approved on the test account first.
- **PR #37 review (Codex):** P1, the owner's notification checked that the target's number is nobody's lead only when the owner recorded it, so a CRM contact saved or imported later with that number (exactly or by its last eight digits), or a conversation it opened, would have been told: the send now applies the recording's own test before it waits or calls and blocks the notification `target_held` (`20261020140000_owner_target_recheck.sql`, `ownerNotifications.dbtest.ts`, red on the earlier definition).

### E. The browser inbox: see, reply, release (decision 6)

Decision S follows from decision 6: the browser may cause the send of a person's own reply. One OD-8a migration adds three functions (ADR 0019: 24 functions, eight acts; its Decisions 2 and 3 unchanged).
- **`get_conversation(ref)`,** on explicit open, `no-store`, memory only: the last 50 turns *(delegated)*, inbound as the lead's raw text (a person reading is not AI processing), a refused message as the refused-media marker, only sent replies; the holder, the first name, the window's deadline, a revision token. Text only for synthetic or test task classes; plain text. `get_review` still shows an unsent AI draft (ADR 0023 §L): a draft a member pastes into the composer is the member's own act, sent as a person's reply.
- **`reply_to_conversation(ref, text, expected_revision)`,** in one transaction: requires a person holder, a current revision (otherwise answered `stale`, nothing recorded; refusals are answer states), an anchor (§A) and a second factor verified within the hour (the auth provider's per-session records, `auth.mfa_amr_claims`, for a factor the user had before the session began); validates 1 to 2000 characters; opens the person review (source `company-os-ui`) accepted by the principal; requests the send (`person_reply`); queues one `outbound.reply_send` job. The table guard admits it only for a review the same principal accepted in that act; the browser never retries. A reply resolves nothing; the release does.
- **`release_conversation(ref, expected_revision)`** calls the existing release; refused while an opt-out is open. A stale revision and an open opt-out are answer states (`stale`, `opt_out_open`), never the existing release's OS409.
- **The screen** "Fila de atendimento" (pt-BR): the list, the conversation, a composer (memory only) and "Devolver à IA"; "na fila" until the worker settles. Takeover, exception acts, AI drafts and marks stay CLI-only.

**Built in slice 5** (migrations `20261021120000_browser_inbox_acts.sql`, every callee and the replaced send functions, and `20261021130000_company_os_browser_inbox.sql`, the fifth exact OD-8a file; SI-87 added, SI-21, SI-45, SI-48, SI-49, SI-52, SI-56, SI-58, SI-74, SI-81 and SI-82 amended, their final texts in docs/SECURITY_INVARIANTS.md; [ADR 0019](0019-company-os-operator-surface.md) "Decision S"). An adversarial review of the design (26 findings, each verified) changed it before any code. Together they reconciled these points:
- **The reference** *(delegated)* is `p_task_id`: any task of the conversation's inbound messages, within the caller's tenant; another tenant's task, an unknown one or a task of no conversation answers OS404 exactly like a random uuid. The open and both acts serve only a conversation the inbox lists (an open `person_requested` or `message_waiting` episode), so a task id from another screen never opens one it does not list.
- **Refusals are answer states, never access errors** *(delegated)*. The read answers `available`, `withheld` (`not_test`, `erased`) or `not_waiting`. The reply answers `queued`, `already_recorded`, `second_factor_required`, `withheld`, `not_held`, `not_waiting`, `stale`, `not_sendable` (with the send gate's reason), `nothing_to_answer`, `content_erased` or `reply_limit`; the release `released`, `already_with_agent`, `withheld`, `not_waiting`, `stale` or `opt_out_open`. Only `queued` and `released` write anything. Errors are left for a malformed request (OS400), an unknown reference (OS404), the identity gate, a lock wait past 2 s (OS429) and the unexpected (OS500), so a stale revision never signs a member out. This corrects §E, which first answered the reply's stale revision with OS409, and its "(the token's `amr`)".
- **The recent second factor is the auth provider's own record, not the token's `amr` claim** (nothing in the repository read `amr`). `ops.operator_second_factor_recent()` requires, for the caller's session (`session_id` and `sub` of the verified claims), a `totp` row of `auth.mfa_amr_claims` updated within the hour (GoTrue v2.196.0 upserts it on each verification of the session, as read from its binary; and, measured on the local provider by `supabase/tests/companyOsProbe/stepUpChecks.mjs`, it also moves on a session already at aal2, keeping its level and id, so the browser asks for a code) and that the session's own factor (`auth.sessions.factor_id`) is a verified TOTP factor of that user created before the session began. A stolen AAL2 session could otherwise enrol a factor whose secret it owns and verify it at will, since enrolment needs only aal2; a member who enrolled during this session signs in again. The review of the built slice measured the limit: a factor the stolen session enrols counts in a later session, which the thief can open by setting a new password through it (the auth provider asks for no current password); binding the step-up to a factor the owner acknowledged is a follow-up, and the inbox sends only to test numbers meanwhile (SI-47). Only SI-74's non-production row waives it. It is read before the reference, so a member without it learns nothing about any conversation; the browser asks for the code inline and the member confirms the send again. The release needs no recent factor.
- **The send** is a new kind, `person_reply`, carried by the same `outbound.reply_send` job under every gate of a fixed text but the published-policy check: the exact contact and its flag (never relaxed), the opt-out rule, the window, the stale rule, redaction and the kill switch. Its bound is the contact's window (`fresh_until` = the last message + 24 hours): `outside_service_window` once it passes, or `transport_not_configured` when no worker carries a transport. No hourly cap applies (the cap ends loops between automatic responders; a person's replies are bounded by five per message and revision), and neither kind counts against the other.
- **The act refuses up front what the send would refuse now** (`not_sendable` with the reason), with nothing written; an execution stop does not refuse it: the request is recorded and the job is held, and the turn reads `held` ("Retida por uma pausa") while a stop of its unit holds it.
- **One reply per click.** The same member asking again for the same text at the same revision is answered `already_recorded` and writes nothing, unless that send failed or was blocked; the composer also disables its confirmation while the act is pending.
- **Order.** Person replies of one conversation leave in the order they were written (the order of their `review:<id>:pending` events, recorded under the conversation's lock with the events sequence uncached); a later one waits, attempt restored, while an earlier one is authorized or sending, and an earlier one the contact's newer message made stale never holds it.
- **The send guard** admits a `person_reply` row only under the act's transaction-local mark, for a person review the same principal accepted in that transaction (`reviewed_at = now()`), with its own queued reply job and the window as its bound. `ops.request_outbound_send` is not replaced: its `carried_by_job` refusal already keeps the operator's send off a send a job carries.
- **What the read shows.** Text only for a test channel, a number not erased and a newest message of synthetic or test class, each turn checked again on its own task. The last 50 turns *(delegated)*, oldest first, with `earlierTurns`: an admitted message's text as the contact wrote it, digits included (no field carries the number or an identifier; SI-87 says exactly that); a refused message as its reason; a reply once it left or may have left, and a person's reply in any state, with its author, fixed key, whether it left on its own (`automatic`), its delivery and its reason. The "Texto fixo publicado" mark (§B) lands there, not on the review page: a new key in `get_review`'s strict schema would break the deployed frontend between the database and frontend deploys. The first word of the contact's CRM first name (letters, hyphens and apostrophes, at most 40, never stored), the holder, the window's end, the revision, and `allowedActs` with `replyUnavailable`, which the acts decide again. The conversation is read again every 15 seconds while it is open, into memory only.
- **The release** calls `ops.release_conversation` unchanged, so a member's release closes `person_requested`, `configuration_missing` and `message_waiting`, as the owner's does: the inbox makes no exception act, and its release closes what the owner's release closes. A reply already queued still leaves after the release, and a message whose screening was still pending is screened with the agent holding; the confirmation text says both.
- **Lock order.** The acts take the conversation `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, then its state `FOR UPDATE`; the screening takes the state, then the conversation `FOR KEY SHARE` only, which does not conflict with `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, so the two never wait on each other in a cycle. Migration `20261021120000`'s end state pins that the screening takes no stronger lock on a conversation and never updates one.
- **A slice 3 defect, fixed here because the inbox would make it reachable from the browser:** the opt-out branch of `ops.sync_send_exceptions` now keys on `author = 'fixed'`, so a person's own reply anchored on an opt-out message never brings the CRM's opt-out record forward when it settles.
- **The owner's tools are unchanged.** `front-desk reply` shares the review logic (`ops.open_person_reply_review`) and keeps its source `operator-cli` and its answer; `messaging send` never carries a `person_reply` send.
- **Review of the built slice** (five lenses, each finding verified; eight confirmed, all P2). Answered before the PR: a person's reply now waits until every message of its conversation, the one it answers included, is screened (`ops.person_reply_message_unscreened` in `begin_reply_send`), so an opt-out or a crisis in it is seen before the reply leaves (`browser_inbox.sql` G10, mutation-checked); a reply that left without a draft reads `withheld`; the act and the contract refuse the C1 control characters alike; the browser tells a provider it could not ask from an account with no factor, and gives the focus back when a confirmation closes; two vacuous checks (G5, whose SQL `or` could carry the later reply first, and B8) and the invariant markers that matched comments were fixed. Recorded, not changed: the second-factor limit above, and the turns' two clocks (D10: a message the store refused stands at its event's instant, an admitted one at the provider's).
- **Deployment order:** database, then frontend. No worker or gateway change (the reply handler's change is a comment); an older frontend never calls the new functions. Staging needs slices 2 to 4 first; the owner's live test is recorded in CURRENT_STATE.
- **Residuals:** a reply or release during a send to the same conversation answers busy after 2 s (that send holds the conversation through its call); recency narrows token theft and does not remove it; the send guard binds application paths, not the database owner; a conversation a person holds with no waiting episode (a takeover with no new message, the hourly cap's hold, an opt-out's hold) stays with the owner's tools, and an open opt-out blocks both acts, its dismissal or resolution staying an owner act.

### F. No booking after a crisis (decision 2, delegated)

- **The hold:** `ops.conversation_states.scheduling_hold`, each change recorded once; set only by a crisis screening; never expires; cleared only by the owner's recorded act `front-desk -- scheduling-hold release`; never in the CRM, an event or a notification.
- **No crisis signal to the model:** while held, availability reads `unavailable` with no slots; a hold-caused acknowledgement reaches later turns only as the marker (§A). A model may infer that scheduling is closed, never why.
- **A booking request goes to a person:** screen v4 (pack v5) adds a lexical `schedulingRequested` (agendar, marcar, remarcar, vaga, encaixe, horário). Under the hold it is a request for a person (`person_requested`), answered `human_handoff_ack` and recorded on the screening as hold-caused.
- **The guard:** an `ENABLE ALWAYS` guard, `ops.create_booking` and `ops.reschedule_booking` refuse a held conversation's booking (OS409 `scheduling_held`) for every writer, the owner included; an earlier booking is untouched; every future booking path carries `conversation_id`. A lexical false positive holds scheduling until the owner clears it.

### G. The site's triage (decision 5)

- **Detection, always:** a deterministic parser finds the marker ("fiz a triagem no seu site") configured in a fifth kind, `intake_form`, never in the model's context; the database derives `intakeReceived` from intake rows of the run's own data class; prompt `lead_triage.v6` says only: do not ask again or mention it.
- **Answers, kept for the owner:** templates compile from the form (escaped literals, options, an integer range, one bounded text); a mismatched field is missing, never the nearest option; partial counts as received. `ops.intake_answers` keeps them with the task's data class, no fingerprint, an `ENABLE ALWAYS` guard, nulled at the task's redaction, read by no application role; values show only for synthetic or test rows (`front-desk -- intakes`, "Triagem do site" in `get_review`). Anyone can type the template; a site wording change stops parsing until a new form version.
- **Tests:** exact parses on the corpus; partial or nothing on edits; bounded time on adversarial input; the leak test on every pack from v2; no value in a screened text, a provider or Jev request, an event or a log.

### I. The crisis conversation (decision 1, the owner's one exception to W1)

The owner wants, besides the fixed texts, a softer and more human conversation that leads a person in crisis to the CVV, and chose it knowing that a model then reads the crisis message ("IA conversa lendo a mensagem", 2026-10-08). It is the one exception to W1, and it is narrow:
- **Order:** the fixed `safety` or `safety_followup` text always goes first, automatically (§B); the conversation follows it and never replaces or delays it.
- **Its own contract,** `crisis_support.v1`, separate from the receptionist's prompt: acknowledge the person and encourage contacting the CVV (188, or its chat) or, in immediate danger, SAMU (192); nothing else: no clinical advice, diagnosis, technique, booking, price, or question about the crisis's details. The output is one reply. A deterministic check refuses a reply without the configured referral, or with a time, price, link or number it was not given; a refused reply is never sent (the fixed text already went).
- **Input:** the crisis message and the conversation's earlier crisis messages, with structured identifiers redacted, and the owner's published crisis guidance. Never the CRM, an intake, the receptionist's context or the waiting list. The receptionist's own context keeps showing those turns as the marker (W1 holds there), and Jev, events, logs, telemetry and exceptions never carry them.
- **Supervised like every AI-written reply:** its draft is a review a person accepts, until the AI-reply autonomy record (decision 3) includes it on evidence.
- **Data:** synthetic and test only. For real data, ADR 0022 bars OpenRouter from `health` (SI-78, unchanged), so the production real-data ADR must name another route (a direct provider with zero data retention, for instance) or leave the conversation off.
- **Limits:** a model in a crisis conversation can misjudge. The fixed text first, the narrow contract, the check and the supervision bound that; the owner does not treat crisis, and the conversation only points to the CVV.
